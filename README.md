# Dubai 3D: interactive 3D map of Dubai (demo)

A real-time 3D model of Dubai, built section by section for real-estate presentations. It now covers five sections, about 21.7 × 19.4 km:
- **Section 1:** Dubai Marina, JBR, Bluewaters, Dubai Harbour, JLT and the Emirates Golf Club area.
- **Section 2:** Palm Jumeirah, Al Sufouh, Media City, Internet City, Knowledge Park, Barsha Heights and Emirates Hills.
- **Section 3:** Al Barsha, Mall of the Emirates, Umm Suqeim, Madinat Jumeirah, Burj Al Arab, Jumeirah Beach Hotel and Kite Beach.
- **Section 4:** Al Quoz, Al Safa, Safa Park, the Dubai Canal, Jumeirah 2-3, Al Wasl and Box Park. It also takes in the western edges of Business Bay (Al Habtoor City, JW Marriott Marquis, Executive Towers) and Dubai Hills (Sidra, Maple, Dubai Hills Mall), plus the sea up to The World's breakwater.
- **Section 5:** Downtown Dubai (Burj Khalifa, The Dubai Mall, the Burj Lake), Business Bay, DIFC, the Sheikh Zayed Road and Trade Centre towers, the Museum of the Future, Al Satwa, City Walk, Dubai Design District, eastern Dubai Hills, Meydan and Global Village. A band along the north adds Jumeirah Bay and the southern islands of The World.

It's inspired by the look of [Isometric Vancouver](https://iso-vancouver.pages.dev/). That map is a flat AI-painted image; this one is a live 3D scene where every building can be clicked, filtered and explored.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://127.0.0.1:5173
npm run build      # static site in dist/ (deploy to Cloudflare Pages, Netlify, S3, ...)
```

The processed map data is already in `public/data/` (`world.json` plus one file per section in `sections/`), so you don't need to fetch anything to run the app.

## What the demo does

| Feature | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Camera** | Orthographic camera at a fixed tilt. The map opens on Dubai Marina, JBR and JLT (about 4.4 km across on a wide screen, 3 km on a phone); zoom out for the whole city. Pan with left-drag, rotate with right-drag, zoom with the wheel (toward the cursor). Q/E rotate in 90° steps, R goes back to the opening view. Every turn pivots on whatever is at the centre of the screen (a facade, a roof or the ground), so the tower you are looking at stays put instead of swinging round a ground point behind it. |
| **3,788 buildings** | Extruded from OpenStreetMap footprints using real heights and floor counts. Includes multi-part towers (Princess Tower crown, Marina 101) and the twisting Cayan Tower. 798 extra low-rise footprints come from Microsoft ML Buildings via Overture Maps. |
| **Look** | A library of facade textures painted in code, chosen per neighbourhood: curtain-wall glass with mullions and spandrels in JLT and the Marina, balcony towers with glass balustrades, JBR sandstone with balcony bands, stone-and-glass, offices, podiums, construction. Glass reflects sky higher up and ground lower down. Roofs have parapets, plant rooms, helipads on some supertalls, AC units, water tanks and podium roof gardens. Lighting: soft shadows, ambient occlusion (N8AO), a crisp sharpen-and-grade pass, animated sea with shallows and foam, calm lakes, 513 yachts on the real pontoons, a rotating Ain Dubai, metro and tram trains. The finish aims at an illustrated map where the colours pop: every block has a warm sunlit side and a deeper, cool shaded side; windows and curtain walls read as vivid teal-to-blue glass (a hue per tower, paler up the tower where it mirrors the sky); walls come in warm stone, sand and peach with a few terracotta accents; silhouettes carry a dark ink line and contact shadows are deep. Colour comes from the palette and the light, not from global saturation, which stays moderate. Lawns are a natural deep green with wind gusts rolling across them (bands of bent, lighter blades in the land shader), and flower beds along lawn edges (a dark clipped hedge packed with pink, magenta, white and red blooms, planted by the land shader in bands the ground painter marks, so they stay sharp at any zoom). |
| **Hand-modelled landmarks** | Shapes come from published descriptions (`src/layers/landmarks.js`). Cayan Tower: hexagonal plates twisting 90° in titanium frames. Princess Tower: observation drum, mosque-style dome and 22 m spire. 23 Marina: octagon with converging white ribs. Elite Residence: ribbed crown, dome and spire. The Torch: three sections and a twisting glass "flame". Ocean Heights: tapering, leaning shaft with a slanted roof. DAMAC Heights: elliptical blade with a glazed summit. Marina 101: 45 m sky-blue faceted crown with a four-point pinnacle. Address Beach Resort: twin towers and a sky bridge with the infinity pool. Almas Tower: sliced top and mast. Uptown Tower 1: faceted massing and steel crown. Ciel: sky deck and rooftop pool. Emirates Crown: crown fins. Also Pier 7. Crowns and domes glow and cycle colour at night. Buildings whose OSM parts don't cover the outline get a reconstructed podium. |
| **Villas & neighbourhoods** | Styled from aerial imagery. The Meadows: cream villas with stepped flat roofs and parapets. The Springs: white townhouses with dark roof decks and red-brick drives. Jumeirah Islands: terracotta hip roofs (plus contemporary villas). Every villa gets a walled plot with a lawn, a pool and deck when it has one, a driveway with a parked car and garden planting. Streets have green verges, sidewalks, street lamps that glow at night, and zebra crossings only where OpenStreetMap maps a pedestrian crossing (about 3,900 across the map; none are generated at junctions). Parking follows how Dubai lots appear in Esri World Imagery (Oct 2026). Only genuine surface lots qualify: underground and multi-storey car parks are excluded, along with one OSM "lot" that is really sand. About 8,700 marked bays line up perpendicular to each lot's aisles, in red interlocking pavers, light paving or asphalt with white lines. About 30% of lots have shade canopies. Lots are 30–70% occupied, and villa cars sit in the forecourt by the front door. An automated check confirms no parked car overlaps a road, ~2,650 umbrellas and loungers line JBR beach, and ~48,700 trees include date palms, broadleaf trees, shrubs and bougainvillea. Palms line the Marina Walk and the lakes, and towers stand in landscaped grounds. |
| **Roads & flyovers** | Heights are solved over the whole road and rail network at once (`scripts/lib/elevation.mjs`). OSM `layer` only orders crossings, so every shared node gets one height. Each of the 232 grade-separated crossings pins the upper deck at least 6.5–8 m above the lower one, and grade-limited ramps (6% roads, 3.5% metro) climb the connected ways. The 4-level SZR interchanges connect without steps. Approach ramps on ordinary ways get earth-filled retaining walls below ~6.5 m, and continue on piers above that. |
| **Metro & tram** | The Red Line is drawn as photographed (Wikimedia Commons). It runs on one twin-track box girder per corridor (`scripts/lib/viaduct.mjs` merges OSM's separate track lines), with a cantilevered deck, sloped webs and parapets, on single round columns with tapered hammerhead caps that are kept out of the carriageways. Stations (DMCC, Sobha Realty, Al Fardan Exchange) sit at platform level ~15 m. Each has a long, low, lens-shaped champagne shell dotted with dark vents, dark glazing under the eave, and a glazed concourse on columns. Enclosed skybridges run at concourse level to entrance pods. 5-car trains in the white/navy/blue livery brake into stations, dwell and pull away, and trams do the same at their stops. At night the stations are floodlit and the windows glow. |
| **2026 vegetation** | NDVI comes from a cloud-free Sentinel-2 scene (8 Nov 2026, `scripts/fetch-ndvi.py`). It paints lawns, verges, roundabouts and the landscaping OSM never mapped under the crisp OSM parks and plots, and places ~9,900 extra trees and shrubs where the index shows dense planting. |
| **Living ground** | Grass: real blades (`src/layers/grass.js`) grow on every lawn round the view from about zoom 12, bend in the same gusts that roll over the painted ground, and take the colour painted under them; a fixed pool of tufts wraps round the view, so nothing swims. They keep off paving, buildings, kerbed streets, motorways and lakes (a flag in the ground's class map). Sand: low dunes with sharp crests and shaded slip faces, ripples that creep downwind, and streamers of blowing sand in gusts. Sea: a rolling swell that arrives in sets and breaks into whitecaps offshore, with calm slicks between ruffled patches. All shader work on the existing ground, so it costs almost nothing. |
| **Pools** | Public and villa pools share one material (`makePoolMaterial` in `ground.js`): a pale shallow end with steps sloping to a deeper blue end, mosaic joints, a band of darker waterline tiles, lane lines on pools over 22 m, moving caustics, and a glow from below at night. Public pools get a band of stone coping. |
| **Clouds** | About one heaped cumulus per 3.3 km2, plus small fair-weather puffs and thin translucent veils of mist, at 170-780 m. A few are kept near Burj Khalifa and Marina 101, so cloud usually passes through their upper floors. Heaps and puffs cast moving shadows; veils do not. Clouds turn see-through from zoom 2 and are gone by 7.5. |
| **Traffic trails** | 6,000 cars and 160 buses, weighted toward the motorways and main roads. Each leaves a short trail that follows the road's curve: two pale speed lines by day, a red tail-light smear at night. |
| **Bounce light** | The foot of every wall picks up the colour of the ground a few metres in front of it (read from the ground painting), fading with height: green by a lawn, warm by sand. A cheap stand-in for global illumination; daytime only. |
| **Night** | Window lights keep their sharpness at every zoom: single windows up close (each with its own tone, some with a curtain part-way across), merging into 2x2, 4x4... blocks that light together as they shrink below a pixel, so a tower is always a crisp mosaic and never grey speckle. Homes are scattered warm rooms; office towers run cool white with some floors lit end to end. Roads glow as warm ribbons, paving and lawns keep a hint of their colour, lower floors catch the street's glow, glass carries a faint sheen of night sky. The glow pass is a tight halo on the brightest lights only. On the water: no foam, crests or sparks after dark, just a soft warm glow along lit shores. Clouds thin out at night, and the corners of the picture fall away slightly. |
| **Interface** | H (or the eye button) hides every panel and leaves the map alone; H, Esc or the small corner button brings them back. The filter panel and the time bar each collapse to a pill (remembered in `dubai3d.ui.v1`). Below 900 px the toolbar shows icons only; below 640 px the details card becomes a bottom sheet. The FPS meter is off unless switched on in Layers or the URL has `?debug`. |
| **JLT** | It follows aerial imagery. Each tower stands on a generated 3–4 level parking and retail podium (pulled back from roads, neighbours and the water) with a landscaped deck, palms and often a pool. The lakes are ringed by paved, palm-lined promenades and the plazas between clusters are paved. Glass is cool-tinted (blue, grey, green, teal, indigo). |
| **Balconies** | Balcony-type towers (incl. JBR) have real projecting slabs and reflective glass balustrades on every floor, with open corners, so their facades have depth and contact shadow. |
| **Walkable streets** | All 1,700+ mapped walkways are drawn as real surfaces: stone-paver footways with kerbs, wide promenades with accent bands, timber boardwalks, stairs, and shared foot/cycle paths with a red cycle strip and bike symbols. A beach promenade with a rubber running track follows the landward edge of every sandy beach (JBR / The Beach), with lifeguard towers along the waterline and white shade sails over the busiest promenades. 2,400 slim lanterns line the lively walkways and glow at night. 216 outdoor dining terraces spill out of mapped restaurants, cafes and bars onto the walkway in front, with rooftop dining on some pavilions. Bike-share docks are mapped too. Each walkway has a liveliness score from nearby venues, shops, beach and marina. It drives the crowds: thousands of pedestrians with swinging arms and legs, joggers, cyclists, sunbathers on the loungers and swimmers in the shallows. |
| **Parks & amenities** | Mapped OSM amenities are rendered with their real markings and furniture. Tennis and padel courts have nets, fences and padel glass; basketball courts have hoops; football pitches have goals; there is beach volleyball and the running track. Playgrounds get poured-rubber floors, play towers with slides, swings, spring riders, climbing domes and tensioned shade sails. Fountains have animated jets. Also mapped: 440+ benches (plus benches along the Marina Walk), sculptures, outdoor gym frames, shelters and waving UAE flags. Cafe terraces with parasols face the water on the Marina Walk and the JLT lakes. Large parks without a mapped playground get an illustrative one. |
| **Sea & beach** | Breakwaters and revetments (the Palm's crescent, harbour moles, groynes) are built of small low-poly armour stones (0.6-1.1 m), stacked in two layers. On a shore the walk's side is a hard concrete kerb with the stones lying below it; they slope to the waterline and carry on under a veil of the sea's own water, clear at the rock and deepening over a few metres, the way the sea meets the sand on the beaches. The land is cut away under the rock and there is no sea wall along those stretches. There would be millions across the map, so they are made on demand in 60 m cells around the view once it is zoomed in past about 5x, and released behind it; from further out the revetment is a mottled grey. The Palm Jumeirah Boardwalk runs along the crescent behind them as a 9 m red rubber-surfaced promenade with joggers, from OpenStreetMap (most of it is tagged `bridge=boardwalk`, which the build used to drop with the footbridges). Apple Maps satellite imagery was used as a visual reference only for both; nothing is traced from it. The water is graded by depth: turquoise shallows with a sandy bottom and caustics along the beaches, mid blue in the harbours, deep navy a kilometre out, with surf lines breaking into lacy foam at the sand. Painted wavelets (short bright crests and soft troughs) drift across all the water, and the water glows faintly with its own colour by day, so the marina stays turquoise in the towers' shadows. The beach has damp sand and a glossy swash line. On the water: jet skis carving loops, kitesurfers under arc kites, surfers and paddleboarders, and motor yachts, speedboats and sailboats, all leaving foam wakes. The playful craft head in after dark. |
| **Night** | A realistic share of lit windows (a quarter to a third) varied in colour temperature, with lit shopfronts and a warm glow at street level. Facades keep a faint lift so the massing stays readable. Many towers have LED lines under the parapet, LED balcony edges or vertical LED strips (shown as a soft tint from afar instead of blooming). Street lamps cast warm pools of light, and a blue LED strip runs along the marina edge. Bloom only catches real light sources. |
| **Clouds** | Fair-weather cumulus drift at ~500 m and cast real moving shadows. They thin out as you zoom in. |
| **Sun position** | The compass carries a sun marker at its true azimuth, and the time bar reads e.g. "sun WSW 25°". Changing the time (or the **Sun** toolbar toggle) shows a sun-path dome over the view: a compass ring, today's arc with hour marks, sunrise and sunset, and the sun on its ray. |
| **Street life models** | Low-poly models built in code (`src/layers/vehicles.js`, `people.js`, `marina.js`). Cars come as saloons, SUVs, hatchbacks with a contrast roof, pickups, low sports cars in bright colours and cream Dubai taxis with a coloured roof, plus buses. Bodies are skinned over cross-sections with rounded shoulders and a crowned bonnet, under a raked glasshouse; wheels with alloy rims sit in dark arches, and there are light bars, mirrors and pillars. People are five casual city figures (shirt and trousers, T-shirt and shorts with a backpack, a woman in a top and trousers with long hair and a handbag, a woman in a dress, a man in an open jacket), each with their own top, trouser and hair colour; the mix is illustrative. Berthed boats are hardtop motor yachts (wraparound dark glass under a long white roof with dark panels), sport cruisers and sailing yachts, on a flared hull with a pointed bow, hull windows, a teak toe rail, a stainless bow rail, teak cockpit and swim platform. Clouds are low cumulus at 300-750 m, the height of the tallest towers (about one per 10 km2): they drift among the Marina's supertalls and the upper half of Burj Khalifa, are hidden by towers standing in front of them, and cast moving shadows. |
| **Road tidying** | Where the mapped data makes a grade-separated crossing impossible (a ramp too short to climb between the levels it joins), the height solver sets that crossing aside and re-solves, so one bad ramp no longer drags a whole interchange up to the height limit (10 such crossings; the two ways meet at one level there). Where one mapped segment hands over to the next with a different width, both taper to the mean over 30 m. Lane and edge lines stop where a road meets others (at junction boxes, at a road's end, along a slip road's merge), as painted lines do. Parking aisles are drawn only inside mapped car parks, and short dead-end stubs of service lanes and slip roads are left out. Trees keep clear of what is around them: none stands on or beside a carriageway, inside a junction or under a flyover, and a crown that would reach into a building is shown smaller, as a shrub, or left out. |
| **Trees** | Dubai species: date and Washingtonia palms, the red-orange flame tree (royal poinciana), yellow flame / golden shower, ghaf, neem, conocarpus, frangipani, shrubs and bougainvillea. Each setting has its own mix: villa gardens, community streets, palm-led boulevards, parks, lake edges and the imagery-driven planting. Plants are painted impostors, not meshes (`src/layers/treeAtlas.js`, `trees.js`), in a stylized game-map look. Crowns are dense masses of big round lobes spread evenly over each species' crown shape (best-candidate sampling), over a darker core, each finished in leaf strokes laid over its outer side: lighter and warmer where they face the sky, deeper and cooler underneath, some reaching past the lobe so the outline is made of leaf tips. Each lobe is shaded as its own sphere tilted toward the crown's outward direction, so it reads as a round, lit cluster while the crown still lights as one volume. A tree in flower blooms as one mass in one family of hues (the flame tree orange, the yellow flame golden, bougainvillea magenta) over a green underside; flowering shrubs get dots of flowers instead. Every plant is painted with a dark inked outline. Leafy trees fall into families of green (lime, deep emerald, golden olive), and about one ordinary shade tree in six is shown in flower (flame tree or yellow flame) so streets and parks carry reds and golds; that share is a styling choice, not data. Palms have broad fronds of fat overlapping leaflets folded in a shallow V, and Washingtonia carry a round head of broad fans. Each species is modelled in 3 variants, and a web worker paints each variant from 3 viewing angles into colour, normal, occlusion and depth layers at load (about 5 s, off the main thread, on the first visit only: the finished atlas is kept in the browser's IndexedDB under its version). Each of the ~48,600 plants is then a single camera-facing card. It picks the painting closest to the view angle and is cel-shaded through the painted normals: four bands (a deep cool shade, mid, sunlit and a small warm highlight) follow the real sun, inner lobes deepen with occlusion, a building's shadow drops the sunlit bands, and a warm rim lights the sunny silhouette. The card writes per-pixel depth so crowns sit properly against buildings and the ground, and casts a silhouette shadow. Crowns sway in travelling gusts, leaves flutter and palm fronds breathe. Date palms beside the lively promenades and the beach track are uplit at night. This cut the trees from ~28 M triangles per frame to under 1 M. |
| **Illustrated finish** | Soft ink outlines on silhouettes and roof edges (from the depth buffer), atmospheric haze toward the far side of the view, vibrance, and split toning (cool shadows, warm highlights). |
| **Sun & time of day** | Uses `suncalc` with Dubai's real sun path for any date and time. The view opens at noon, when the sun stands high in the south: the ground in view is sunlit and the towers' shadows fall mostly behind them. The slider and ▶ button play the whole day. At night the windows, streets, pools and Ain Dubai light up. |
| **Traffic** | About 1,770 cars and buses drive along the real road graph, with headlights at night. |
| **Developer filter** | Choosing a developer (or community) animates the city. The developer's towers pop up with their brand colour on the roofs and a tint on the real facades. The rest of the city fades back but keeps its facades, rippling outward from the selection. **Only show** sinks everything else to its footprint, and clearing the filter raises it again. Communities: Dubai Marina & JBR, Bluewaters, Emaar Beachfront, JLT, and The Meadows & Springs. |
| **Quality** | At the top of the Layers panel: **Auto**, **Low**, **Medium**, **High** or **Ultra**. A preset sets the render resolution, depth shading, night glow, shadow resolution and how often shadows are redrawn, tree density, traffic, whether trees and people cast shadows, and how far around the view each kind of detail is loaded. Auto starts on High and steps down (to Medium, then Low) if the map runs below about 34 fps once it is idle. The choice is remembered. |
| **Layers & FPS** | The **Layers** button (or `L`) opens a panel of switches. City life: traffic, metro & tram, people, boats & water sports, parked cars. Nature: palms, trees, shrubs & flowers, clouds. Streets & places: street lights, parks/beach/amenities, walkways & cycle lanes. Map & display: labels, sun path, depth shading, shadows, night glow and an **FPS counter**. The counter shows frame time, the worst frame, draw calls and triangles, with a 30 s graph. **Clean view** hides everything that moves, and **Show everything** brings it all back. Hidden layers also stop animating. Choices are remembered between visits. |
| **Palm Jumeirah** | The trunk's apartment blocks, the 16 fronds of villas with terracotta or flat roofs, pools and private beaches, and the crescent's resorts. Hand-modelled landmarks: **Atlantis The Palm** (coral wings, the twin Royal Towers with domed crowns and spires, the arch with the Royal Bridge Suite on top), **The Palm Tower** (glass shaft with the cantilevered crown carrying the 360° Aura Skypool and The View) and **Atlantis The Royal** (KPF's stacked blocks from OSM, with the sky-bridge infinity pool). The **Palm Monorail** runs on twin straddle beams from Palm Gateway along the trunk and across the lagoon to Atlantis, with 3-car trains stopping at its stations. Beaches are classified by who owns the sand: frond villas get private beaches, resorts get rows of umbrellas, and only public beaches get promenades. Running tracks and lifeguard towers stay on the mainland (JBR, Al Sufouh). |
| **Al Barsha, Umm Suqeim & Burj Al Arab** | 29,000 villas in Al Barsha, Al Barsha South and Umm Suqeim, Mall of the Emirates, Madinat Jumeirah and the Al Quoz warehouses. Hand-modelled landmarks: **Burj Al Arab** (the sail on its island, leaning back toward the mast, with the exoskeleton legs, helipad, Al Muntaha and the spire; matte white by day, washed with slowly changing colour at night) and **Jumeirah Beach Hotel** (the breaking wave, stepping up from the beach to its 104 m crest). Beach ownership also honours OSM tags. Kite Beach, Jumeirah Public (Sunset) Beach and Jumeirah Beach are public, with the running track and lifeguards. The Burj Al Arab beach (`access=customers`) is private. |
| **Al Quoz, Al Safa & Jumeirah 3** | A 3 km strip east of Al Barsha, running from Dubai Miracle Garden to the Jumeirah coast. It includes the Dubai Hills villas and mall, the Al Quoz warehouses and Al Khail Gate, and the villas of Al Safa and Jumeirah 3 around Safa Park and the Dubai Canal. It ends at the western edge of Business Bay: Al Habtoor City's Amna, Noora & Meera towers, JW Marriott Marquis (355 m), DAMAC's Safa One and Safa Two, Aykon City, One by Binghatti and the Executive Towers. New communities: Al Quoz, Al Safa & Al Wasl, Jumeirah, and Dubai Hills Estate. |
| **Downtown, Business Bay & DIFC** | **Burj Khalifa** is raised from OpenStreetMap's own 3D model of it: about 35 parts for the three wings stepping back in a spiral round the core. They are glazed with vertical stainless fins, ringed by darker bands at the mechanical floors, stand on stone lobby wings, and finish in a steel pinnacle and a spire to 828 m with a beacon that flashes after dark. **Museum of the Future** is a stainless torus 77 m high on its planted mound, with the "eye" open through it; its skin is cut by flowing calligraphy that is dark glass by day and glows gold at night. Modelled from photographs (Wikimedia Commons, as visual reference only): the **Emirates Towers** (triangular, ribbon-glazed on two faces and aluminium on the third, each top cut by a steep slope rising to twin needles at the corner facing the other tower; the office tower stands on its glass drum lobby), **Address Downtown** (dark glass banded by white floor lines, the crown curling up like a hood to a needle), **Address Boulevard** (white fins and an Art Deco crown of four setbacks with corner needles), the gold crescent of **Address Dubai Mall**, **Index Tower** (concrete end cores, louvred glass), **Rose Rayhaan**, **Gevora** (gold glass, stepped crown), **Il Primo**, **Al Yaqoub Tower** (modelled on Big Ben, with clock faces lit at night), **Dusit Thani** (the inverted Y of hands joined in greeting), the 1979 **World Trade Centre tower**, One Za'abeel's **The Link** with its cantilevered pool, and **Dubai Opera** (a dhow, its glass prow facing the Burj). About 60 other towers get their facade type and glass tone from photographs. **The Dubai Mall** has stone arcades and the roof seen from the Burj: long white ribbed barrel vaults, glass domes (one gilded), the star atrium and fields of pyramid lights, all glowing from inside at night. The **Burj Lake** is the bright turquoise of a shallow lined lake, with the fountain's nozzle rings on it. Souk Al Bahar, the Palace and the **Old Town** are sand-coloured Arabian revival with brown roof decks and wind towers. The Dubai Canal runs through Business Bay. New communities: Downtown Dubai, Business Bay, DIFC & Trade Centre, City Walk, Dubai Design District, Al Satwa & Za'abeel, and Meydan. Dubai Hills Estate now extends east. |
| **Researched communities (Dubai Hills)** | Communities are being rebuilt one at a time from developer floor plans, brochures and photographs instead of from aerial impressions (`data-raw/research/dubai-hills/`, index in its README). **Sidra 1–3** (about 1,250 villas) uses Emaar's E1 3-bed, E3 4-bed and E5 5-bed plans. Each villa is chosen from its footprint's street frontage and mirrored in pairs along each street so neighbouring carports sit side by side, as built. The models have white render with mid-grey picture frames and portal frames, anthracite windows and louvres, teak doors, an open two-car carport at the street corner (about a third closed with garage doors), rooftop AC screens and raised garden terraces. A low white wall with a grey bar railing runs along the street, with 2 m walls round the gardens. Cars stand under the carports and about a third of the gardens have the plans' pool. **Maple 1–3** (about 1,880 townhouses) builds each OSM row as its cluster: four units (end, middle, middle, end) or a pair of end units, Type 2 or 3, from the brochure plans. Upper bedroom boxes cantilever over open 6 m carports, parapets are staggered and the frontages are open. Maple 1/2 is sand render with charcoal surrounds; Maple III is white with grey. **Club Villas** and **Golf Grove** (about 180 semi-detached pairs) use Emaar's TH01/TH02 plans: mirror units with a deeper first floor overhanging two open carports under one white canopy, and glass-railed roof terraces with stair boxes. Club Villas is greige with teak panels and a louvred recess between the units. Golf Grove has three designs: Eames (beige and grey with timber and louvre screens), Savoye (a white frame and a brown clad box) and the 4-bed Aalto (windows alternating with timber panels between grey bands). **Golf Place** (about 170 villas) builds two wings joined by a double-height glazed entrance under a pergola, with the garage terrace on top, in its three styles: Contemporary (dark bronze framed glass boxes with a white mullion grid, travertine walls), Modern (white cubes) and Elegant (floating roof slabs with deep eaves, white louvres, grey stone). Golf Place has low travertine walls along the street and long pools in about half the gardens. **Fairway Vistas** and **Parkway Vistas** (about 130 golf-front villas) are built on the brochure plans, measured room by room: B1 (7 bed: a near-square first floor with a notch over a planted entrance court, the carport tucked under the first floor at the front left) and B2 (6 bed: two staggered wings joined by a family room, the garage at the front right) in both, and at Parkway also B3 (H-shaped first floor, open parking) and B4 (the largest: two long wings round the pool court, a covered carport out in front). No plot-by-plot type schedule was published, so the type comes from the footprint's proportions. Each has its façade options: Modern (white render, thin roof slabs with deep overhangs, travertine panels, bronze fins, glass-railed terraces), Arabesque (beige boxes in deep picture frames with bronze geometric screens) and, for B3/B4, Classic (cornices, framed windows, a tall entrance portal). Fronts are open lawns, with a low slatted fence along the golf course and the garden plan's pool behind. ## Building Dubai section by section

The city is split into sections (`scripts/sections.json`). Each one is a set of lat/lon rectangles with a name and the areas it covers, and all sections share one fixed projection origin, so a built section never moves. The map covers the union of the built sections (for now it has to form a rectangle). Section 3 includes the open sea north of the Palm for this reason, Section 4 is a full-height strip (25.06–25.205 N) plus the sea north of Umm Suqeim. Section 5 is another full-height strip (55.265–55.315 E) plus a full-width band to 25.235 N. The home view frames the whole diorama.

**Streaming.** The pipeline writes `world.json` and one file per section:
- **`world.json` (13 MB, always loaded):** the coast, water, roads and the traffic graph, rail, districts and berths, plus a light record of every building. Light records have simplified outlines but keep their tower parts, and landmarks keep their full model.
- **Section files (3–16 MB):** the detail, made of full buildings, trees, villa plots, parking, street furniture, amenities and walkways.

How streaming works:
- **Start-up:** the whole city is drawn at once from the light records, cut into 1 km chunks. Search, developer filters and the explorer therefore work everywhere from the start. The scene shows after about 4.5 s, against about 20 s for the old single 30 MB file.
- **Section load:** a section's file is fetched when the view comes near it. Its data is then split into the same 1 km cells.
- **Cell parts:** the cells near the view are built part by part: detailed buildings, trees, gardens and parked cars, walkways and street furniture, amenities, and people.
- **Order and pacing:** parts are built nearest first. Each is a short job of a few milliseconds, and jobs pause while you pan or zoom. A cell's detailed buildings replace its light chunk exactly.
- **Range limits:** how far each part reaches depends on the quality preset. Parts too small to see when zoomed far out are skipped: people, street furniture, and detailed buildings. Trees thin out when zoomed far out, but they then reach across the whole view (up to three times the preset's radius) and the ones left are drawn larger, so a far view keeps the canopy of the close one. Detailed buildings always cover a view that fits within 2.6 km, on every preset.
- **Unloading:** parts that leave their reach are disposed after a few seconds, and a section's data is released 30 s after none of it is wanted.

Rendering cost:
- **Culling:** off-screen cells, tree cards and crowds are culled.
- **Shadows:** the shadow map is redrawn when the view or the sun moves. Otherwise it is redrawn at the preset's rate, not every frame.
- **Ground:** the ground's base painting and its sharp ~1.2 km detail tiles are painted on a GPU canvas and copied straight into GPU textures, so they never take a CPU round trip. Tiles are painted on demand into a fixed pool.

Measured on this machine with three sections, before section 4 was added:
- **Main-thread stalls:** the longest stall while detail streams in is about 65 ms. The old whole-section build froze for up to 1.2 s.
- **Zoomed-in view:** detailed geometry is about a tenth of what loading everything built (0.7 M of 6.2 M building vertices).
- **Debug switch:** `?stream=off` shows the light city only.

With five sections, world.json is 27.9 MB and the scene appears after about 6 s. The browser console prints how long each start-up step took (`[startup]`). The biggest step is the ground, at about 2 s: the base painting, the shore-distance texture and the vegetation grid. The shore blur and the community lookups were sped up to keep start-up near 6 s.

## Data pipeline

```bash
npm run fetch-data   # OSM for every built section, from the OpenStreetMap API in cached tiles -> data-raw/osm.json,
                     # plus the amenities and transit stations it contains -> data-raw/amenities.json, stations.json
pip install overturemaps && python -m overturemaps download --bbox=<w,s,e,n of the built sections> -f geojson -t building -o data-raw/overture-buildings.geojson
python -I scripts/fetch-ndvi.py   # Sentinel-2 NDVI for the built sections -> data-raw/ndvi.bin + public/data/ndvi.png (pip install rasterio numpy pillow)
npm run build-data   # -> public/data/world.json + public/data/sections/<id>.json
```

The public Overpass servers were overloaded when section 2 was fetched, so `fetch-osm.mjs` reads the OpenStreetMap API instead (small `/map` tiles that split themselves when they hold too many nodes). Afterwards it completes multipolygons that reach beyond the tiles. Section 1's earlier Overpass extract is reused from its cache.

`scripts/build-data.mjs` does the following:
- projects to local metres from the fixed origin
- builds land polygons from the coastline (the Palm's fronds and crescent are islands)
- assembles multipolygons and attaches building parts
- estimates missing heights
- solves road and rail heights over the whole network (roads, metro, tram and the monorail) and builds the road graph used by traffic
- merges metro tracks into viaduct spines, places piers and generates JLT podiums
- classifies beaches (public, resort, villa) before adding promenades, lifeguards and umbrellas
- plants trees from OSM, street rules and the 2026 NDVI grid
- clips everything to the area
- places trees and berths yachts
- splits the output into `world.json` and one detail file per built section (buildings are ordered section by section, so each section owns a contiguous range of building ids)

## Data sources and honesty notes

- **Geometry:** © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), plus Microsoft ML Buildings via Overture Maps (ODbL). Attribution must stay visible.
- **Vegetation:** Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026 (Sentinel-2 L2A via Element 84 Earth Search). Esri World Imagery and Wikimedia Commons photos were used only as visual references for styling: no data was derived from them.
- **Tower looks:** `data-raw/tower-appearance.json` (researched 2026-11-05) gives facade types and colours for the towers where sources exist. Its facts mostly come from property-portal copy, so it is medium confidence. Every other tower uses its district palette.
